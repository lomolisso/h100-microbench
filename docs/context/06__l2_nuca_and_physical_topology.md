# L2 NUCA, address hashing, and recovering the physical topology from latency

Goal of this context file: give the coding agents (a) what is known about how H100 addresses map onto L2 partitions/slices and DRAM channels, (b) what is known about how SMs map onto GPCs and partitions, and (c) the methodology for turning counters and latency measurements into a topology estimate. The experiments that implement this are `03__sm_gpc_topology` (done), `08__l2_partitions_and_first_touch` (done), `13__l2_slice_mapping`, `14__gmem_latency_vs_controller_distance`, `15__noc_latency_heatmaps`, and for the SM-to-SM side `10__dsmem_latency_heatmap`. Provenance tags: **[MEAS NN]** measured on this device by experiment NN, **[NV]** official, **[PAT]** patent, **[LIT]** published on other chips, **[INF]** inference to confirm.

## 1. Known facts

### 1.1 L2 partitions, sub-nodes, homes and copies
- **Two partitions × two sub-nodes.** Partition 0 = GPCs {G0, G4, G5, G6} with sub-nodes S0 {G0, G6} and S1 {G4, G5}; partition 1 = {G1, G2, G3, G7} with S2 {G1, G2} and S3 {G3, G7}; partner sub-nodes across the partitions are S0↔S3 and S1↔S2 [MEAS 08]. The near/far classification is constant per GPC (each GPC attaches to one partition) [MEAS 08].
- **Hit latency of a line held only at its home**, SM cycles at 1635 MHz, timer pair removed: same partition and sub-node **251**, other sub-node **280** (+29), other partition and partner sub-node **417**, other partition and other sub-node **443** (+162): an additive 2×2 grid [MEAS 08]. Per-SM position term −8…+10 cycles (TPC mates equal; the third TPC of G1–G7 slowest; SMs 92–119 fastest) [MEAS 08]. A minor far mode ≈ 390 cycles affects ≈ 12 % of far reads and is line×SM-specific → slice-level, to be explained by `13`/`15` [MEAS 08].
- **Home = address, not first touch.** Home partition = XOR of physical address bits **12, 13, 15, 17, 19** (100 % linear inside a 2 MiB page) **⊕ 21, 22, 24** (verified where VA = PA in 32 MiB-contiguous groups; bit 23 does not take part; ≥ 25 unresolved); the home is constant per 4 KiB and alternates per 8 KiB; exactly 50/50 [MEAS 08]. The home *sub-node* is not the parity of any single address bit [MEAS 08]. The slice inside the partition is unknown → `13`.
- **Copies.** Any read **or write** of a line from partition P leaves a copy in P (in the partner sub-node of the line's home sub-node); the copy serves every SM of P near; a write from the other partition invalidates it (the next read is far again and returns the new value) [MEAS 08; `05`'s counters agree: a remote store writes 2 L2 sectors per payload sector]. A far first read moves 64 B (2 sectors) over the partition link; near-partition misses are 64 B requests [MEAS 08]. Consequence for every later spec: a **far hit is only observable on the first read after a flush when the line was last touched from the other partition**; a plain chase sees near hits only [MEAS 04]; the home cannot be read off a repeated-access latency.
- **Capacity.** One SM: 100 % near hits up to 26 MiB; 38–52 MiB → 50 % far hits + ≈ 45 % HBM; ≥ 64 MiB → all HBM (misses 505 near / 691 far by home) [MEAS 08]. Streaming: knees at 64–72 MiB (local homes), ≈ 52 MiB (mixed), 40–44 MiB (remote) [MEAS 05].
- **Slices.** `device__attribute_l2s_count` = 96 = `fbp_count` 12 × `num_l2s_per_fbp` 8, but every `lts__*.avg` = `.sum/94` and the HBM bus is 6016 bit = 94 × 64 bit → inference: 2 slices and their 64-bit channels are disabled on this NVL [MEAS 08, INF]. 640 KiB per slice (60 MiB / 96; 50 MB / 80 on the PCIe/SXM SKUs). 8 slices per 512-bit controller [LIT/INF, tested in `13` Step 4]. L2 line 128 B, sector 32 B [NV].
- **Nsight Compute 2025.2.1 on this setup exports no per-slice (per-instance) counter values**, only `.sum/.avg/.min/.max` across units [MEAS 08] → §2.2.
- Prior work on other chips [LIT]: near/far hit 258/414 (H800 PCIe, Luo), 295/470 (H100, DGNA); sub-NUMA +32 (DGNA; ours +29); "first-touch homing" and "replica" (DGNA, Fan et al.) are, on this device, address homing plus the copy above; Jin et al. (H100 PCIe, `08` P6) saw hit latency ≈ 300–340 cycles *uniform across GPCs with no partition step* because their lines were warmed into L2 by the measuring SMs (copies), and a miss penalty bimodal by GPC — both consistent with the mechanism above.

### 1.2 SMs, TPCs, CPCs, GPCs
- 8 GPCs G0–G7 = 12/16/16/16/18/18/18/18 SMs; TPC t = SMs (2t, 2t+1); TPC → GPC: t < 32 → t mod 8; 32–59 → 1 + (t−32) mod 7; 60 → G6, 61 → G7, 62 → G0, 63 → G4, 64 → G5, 65 → G0 [MEAS 03]. SMs 124–131 (TPCs 62–65) only ever pair with their mate and never join clusters ≥ 3; 7 GPCs host CS 16 [MEAS 02/03]. Their GPC assignment rests on clock domains plus one counter constraint, not on co-scheduling → `15`'s slice-signature clustering must confirm it.
- Per-GPC SM clock domains under the lock (≤ 0.76 % apart within a launch) [MEAS 01/03] — convert cycles→ns per GPC when sub-percent matters.
- Logical `%smid` is assigned by the driver [LIT]; the enumeration is round-robin over TPCs of the GPCs (ours: t mod 8 for the first 32 TPCs; Jin et al.'s H100 PCIe: `smid = 14·t + 2·g + {0,1}` for 7 GPCs) [MEAS 03 / LIT].
- **CPC.** The patents describe 3 TPCs = 6 SMs per CPC with a shared GPCMMU/GPCARB [PAT]; Jin et al. measured on an H100 PCIe groups of 2–3 TPCs with identical L2-latency signatures and a DSMEM-latency step from **196** cycles (same CPC) to **213** (CPC2↔CPC2) [LIT]; in their 16-SM GPC the grouping is **CPC = (TPC index within the GPC) mod 3** (TPC index = rank of the TPC among that GPC's TPCs in ascending smid order) → the hypothesis `10` and `15` test on our map [INF].
- TPC mates share the TPCARB/texture path [PAT]; rank pairs (2k, 2k+1) are TPC mates in every first-wave cluster [MEAS 02]; `07` saw all pair types (TPC mates, other TPC, SMs 124/125) within ≈ 55 cycles in flag round trips — whether TPC mates are faster in a dependent DSMEM chase is [INF] (`10`).

### 1.3 NoC observations from Jin et al. (MICRO 2024, V100/A100/H100 PCIe) [LIT; numbers in `08` P6; full digest with the artifact in `docs/research_notes/gpunetbench.md`]
- Method: one thread, one timed `__ldcg` (L1 bypassed with `-dlcm=cg`; not a dependent chain), lines pre-warmed into L2, source SM by `%smid` filter, target slice by address. Slice identification: `nvprof` per-slice events on V100; **on A100/H100 no per-slice counters were available to them either**, so they used a two-kernel **contention oracle** (one kernel hammers one address, the other sweeps addresses; a bandwidth drop = same slice) and mapped 6 of 80 slices on H100. Bandwidth: many CTAs streaming addresses of one slice, measured by `cudaEvent`s or `l1tex__m_xbar2l1tex_read_bytes.sum.per_second`.
- Findings: SM→slice latency is non-uniform by the physical position of the SM in the GPC and of the slice in its memory partition (V100: 175–248 cycles, the slice order is the same for every SM); the per-SM latency signature identifies placement (Pearson correlation heatmap); A100/H100 add the partition step; H100 shows a CPC level (above); **bandwidth from any SM to any slice is uniform** (V100: 34 GB/s per SM→slice, σ 0.15; 85 GB/s per GPC→slice; ≥ 4 SMs saturate one slice; A100 ≈ 8 SMs, ≈ 200 GB/s per slice, far partition up to 28 % lower at 1–2 SMs; H100 single peak ≈ 40–45 GB/s per SM→slice); the NoC provides **input speedup** (H100 read/write: TPC 1.95/2.00, CPC 5.48/4.57, GPC 7.75/8.97 local, 9.1/12.38 global); aggregate L2 fabric bandwidth is 2.4–3.5× the DRAM bandwidth; traffic is load-balanced across slices by the address hash; load-balancing across SMs matters more than across slices (GPC output speedup is partly spatial).
- Caveats for comparisons: single-load timing (no dependency), SM2SM "latency" = ILP-8 loaded latency, GB/s from `prop.clockRate`, their H100 is a PCIe card with 7 GPCs (18/16×6).

### 1.4 DRAM side
- HBM3, 94 GB, 6016-bit bus = 94 × 64-bit channels on 12 FBPAs (one of them 384 bit) [`10`, INF]; read 3.70 / write 3.62 TB/s (writes: bytes that reached HBM during the kernel); one SM reads ≈ 98 GB/s; **half of all HBM traffic crosses the partition link** (0.505 link sectors per HBM sector) [MEAS 05]; miss latency 505 near / 667 far home (+ ≈ 6.5 % slow tail ≈ 856) [MEAS 04/08]; per-GPC dependence of the miss penalty untested beyond SMs 0/2 → `14`.
- No public channel/bank mapping for H100 HBM3 [LIT survey]. GPUHammer's row-buffer-conflict timing method (GDDR6 A6000: 256 B chunks map to one row/bank, successive chunks rotate banks, threshold ≈ 25–30 ns between hit and conflict) is the template; HBM3 has 16 channels × 2 pseudo-channels per stack, 16 banks (4 bank groups) each [INF from the HBM3 spec; accel-sim uses `nbk=16`, `nbkgrp=4`].
- Whether SM→memory-controller distance is visible in DRAM latency is [INF]; the partition split already contributes +162 cycles on a miss [MEAS 08], so the question is whether a *second-order* term (which of the 12 FBPAs) exists (`14`).

## 2. Methodology (synthesised from Jin et al., Luo et al., DGNA, Fan et al., Alpay & Basaran L40, SemiAnalysis Blackwell, Lord of the Ring(s), and our 03/04/05/08)

### 2.1 smid → GPC (and TPC) map — `03__sm_gpc_topology` (done)
1. 16-CTA clusters (non-portable) of a kernel that occupies one CTA per SM (≥ 200 KB dynamic smem) spinning ≈ 100 000 cycles; record `(clusterid, rank, smid)`; repeat for cluster sizes 8, 4, 3, 2 and thousands of launches with different grids; union-find on "same cluster" edges → co-scheduling domains; per-SM clock domains and the `gpc__cgas_launched` rollups as independent signals [MEAS 03: three signals agree].
2. TPC pairs: `%smid` pairs (2k, 2k+1) are TPC mates (100 % of first-wave CS-2 clusters) [MEAS 02/03]; direct evidence (a latency signature) comes from `10`/`15`.
3. Caveat: SMs 124–131 are placed by clock domain; `15` re-tests them through their slice signature; MIG cross-check (1g.12gb = 16 SMs × 7) done.

### 2.2 address → partition / slice / FBPA — `13__l2_slice_mapping` (method decided 2026-10-25, OI-1)
No per-slice instances are exported, so the slice of a line is identified with the **busiest-unit (`.max`) oracle**: in one profiled launch a single thread reads a set of lines with distinct, known repetition counts through dependent `ld.global.cg` chains; `lts__t_sectors_srcunit_tex_op_read.max` is the largest per-unit total, so (a) `.max == .sum` proves that one unit served everything, (b) reading line X and a reference line r_j with distinct counts tells whether they share a unit (`.max` = sum of both counts), and (c) a reference set of one line per slice, read with counts 4096 + 8·j, decodes the slice of up to three test lines (counts 2048/1024/512) per launch; lines that match no reference start a new slice, so the set bootstraps itself and its final size settles 94 vs 96. Home slices are probed from an SM of the line's home partition (home from `08`'s hash); copy slices from an SM of the other partition after a warm read (application replay, caches kept), because a far first read is looked up in both partitions. FBPAs: the same oracle on `fbpa__dram_read_sectors.max` after a flush, reading distinct lines once each. Cross-checks: (A) Jin et al.'s **contention oracle** — 8 SMs stream 256 lines of slice *a* while one SM chases 64 lines of slice *b*; the probe slows down iff a = b (agreement ≥ 99 % on 200 pairs); (B) `08`'s timing classes for the partition; (C) `15`'s per-SM slice signature must be constant within a slice class. Then the hash: GF(2) solve for the partition over bits 7–30 (must reproduce `08`), models for the 48 slices per partition (XOR index + mod-3 group, IPOLY + mod, brute force) with the lookup table as the fallback that `15` consumes, the copy-slice rule as a function of the home slice, and the slice→FBPA grouping. Step 0 of `13` re-checks the instance export once (replay modes, `--set full`, `ncu_report`), and switches to the direct counter method if instances appear.

### 2.3 SM × slice latency, bandwidth and speedup — `15__noc_latency_heatmaps`
- **Latency matrix:** for every SM *i* (one thread, claimed) and every slice *s* (64 lines homed on *s* from `13`, first touched by SM *i* so that no copy ambiguity exists), chase ≥ 1024 dependent hops; median and MAD per (i, s); `.max == .sum` on a sample proves the chase hits only *s*. Fit `L(i, s) = μ + a(i) + b(s)` (+ rank-1 term), report R²; the SM term must split by partition (+162) and sub-node (+29) and the per-TPC position term of `08`; hierarchical clustering of the rows and the **Pearson-correlation heatmap of the per-SM signatures** (Jin et al.) give GPC/CPC groups independently of `03` — the explicit test for SMs 124–131.
- **Per-slice bandwidth (Jin et al. Obs 8/10):** one SM → one slice, one GPC → one slice, N = 1…18 SMs → one slice, for every slice and from both partitions; contiguous (one GPC) vs distributed (one SM per GPC) senders; expected: uniform per-slice bandwidth and saturation with a handful of SMs.
- **Input speedup (Jin et al. Fig. 10):** bandwidth of 1…18 SMs of one GPC added TPC-sorted and CPC-sorted, reads and stores, over a local-home L2-resident buffer; speedup = BW(N)/BW(1) at the TPC, CPC and GPC levels.
- **Loaded latency vs injection (GPUNetBench L2_LAT-BW):** all SMs, 2 CTAs/SM, 1…1024 threads/CTA, one timed dependent load per thread at a fixed iteration, stream and random patterns, per-warp random delay variant → latency–throughput curve.
- Interpretation rule (AnandTech caveat): an SM→SM latency measured *through L2* is a 3-point path (SM_i → slice → SM_j) and must not be read as a distance between the SMs; use DSMEM for true 2-point SM↔SM latency.

### 2.4 SM × SM DSMEM latency matrix — `10__dsmem_latency_heatmap`
- Cluster size 16 and 8 so that every pair inside a GPC co-occurs; rank *r* chases into rank *r'* via `mapa` + `ld.shared::cluster` (dependent chain, 1 thread), serialised across source ranks with `cluster.sync()`; also the 2-point store→flag→load ping-pong (`st.async` + mbarrier) for one-way latency; ≥ 1000 samples per pair; bin by `(smid_src, smid_dst)` across launches; order rows/columns by the §2.1 map. Reference: 184–218 cycles, flat on other cards [LIT]; H200 one-way 78 cycles [LIT].
- **Structure tests:** same-TPC pairs; same-CPC pairs under the hypothesis CPC = (TPC index within the GPC) mod 3 and under every other partition of the GPC's TPCs into groups of 3 (pick the grouping minimising within-group variance, report the F-statistic); Jin et al.'s step is ≈ 17 cycles (196 → 213) on H100 PCIe, so ≥ 20 launches × 4096 hops per pair (MAD ≤ 2 cycles) are needed; also the ILP-8, 1024-thread **loaded** per-request latency of the GPUNetBench ONE2ONE design as a secondary matrix. A significant difference (> 3 MAD) is evidence of a hierarchical fabric; none means a single-stage crossbar at the resolution of `%clock64`.

### 2.5 DRAM channel distance — `14__gmem_latency_vs_controller_distance`
- Fine-grained p-chase on a ≥ 1 GB buffer with every access missing (random order, flush between repetitions), per-line latency classes correlated with (a) the home partition (`08` hash), (b) the FBPA from `13` Step 4 (`fbpa__dram_read_sectors.max` oracle), (c) address bits 8–20 (channel interleave candidates); row-buffer-conflict pairs (GPUHammer method) identify same-bank lines. Per-GPC miss-penalty matrix (8 probe SMs × near/far homes) tests Jin et al.'s bimodal miss penalty. Prior expectation: the partition term dominates; a controller term, if any, is small [INF].

### 2.6 Statistics and reporting
- Per-pair medians with MAD; ≥ 1000 samples for matrices, ≥ 100 per line for maps; report clock, SKU, driver.
- Always keep raw per-sample CSV rows so the notebooks can re-cluster.
- Jitter reference: 0.09 cycles median temporal jitter over 6 h at full load for an SM×region signature (RTX 5090) [LIT] — differences of a few cycles are real if sample counts are adequate.
